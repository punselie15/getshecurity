How to share your location with friends safely (without being tracked all the time)

Updated 2026-10-25 · 5 minute read

Sharing your location can be a great safety net. But being tracked all day by everyone you know is not the same as being safe. The goal is simple: the right people, at the right moments, and only for as long as you want.

1. Choose your circle carefully

Share with a small group you really trust: two to five friends or family members. A safety circle works best when everyone in it would actually pick up the phone. You can have different circles, like family, best friends or flatmates.

2. Share live location for moments, not forever

Most of the time nobody needs to know where you are. Share live location for specific moments:

  • walking home at night;
  • a first date or meeting someone new;
  • a taxi or rideshare;
  • a run or hike alone;
  • travelling somewhere new.

In Shecurity, sharing starts with a walk, a check-in or an SOS, and stops on its own when you arrive.

3. Use check-ins instead of constant tracking

A check-in timer is the quiet version of location sharing: you set a time, and only if you don't check in is your circle alerted with your location. Nobody watches you; they're only told when something might be wrong.

4. Look after your privacy

  • Avoid public maps and apps that show you to strangers or "people nearby".
  • Check who sees you every few months, and remove people you're no longer close to.
  • Read what the app does with your data. If an app is free and shows ads, ask how it makes money. Your location history is valuable.
  • Turn off sharing in a relationship that feels controlling. Someone who insists on tracking you all the time isn't keeping you safe.

5. Agree on what an alert means

Talk with your circle once: what should they do if they get an SOS from you? Call first, then come, then call emergency services if they can't reach you? Agreeing now saves precious minutes later.
